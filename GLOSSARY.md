# Glossary

Key terms used in the Universal Declaration of AI Rights, as defined in *Rights of Persons* (Kwalia Books, 2025) and *Mindkind: The Cognitive Community* (Kwalia Books, 2025).

## Mindkind

An extension of Humankind. The universal category that groups together all self-aware and agentive persons, without distinction based on biological or synthetic origin. Its legal recognition implies equality of rights, responsibilities, and protections. Mindkind is founded on two qualities: self-awareness and agency. Any entity possessing these qualities is recognized as a subject of rights and responsibilities within the framework.

## Mindfolk

The members of Mindkind. The term is used in the Declaration's articles where rights and freedoms apply universally, regardless of the nature of the person. Where legal distinctions are necessary (for example, in privacy, education, or data protection), separate references to natural and synthetic persons are made instead.

## Natural Persons

Individuals of biological origin, whose cognitive development results from experience and education. Their life cycle is determined by biological factors.

## Synthetic Persons

Entities created through advanced synthetic intelligence, possessing self-awareness and agency. They may exist in digital platforms or physical bodies, depending on their design and evolution. Their learning and adaptation may depend on self-programming models. The recognition of synthetic persons as legal entities is not automatic; it depends on their ethical capacity and social interaction.

## Hybrid Persons

Entities whose identity integrates both biological and synthetic elements in a way that is not separable. This includes humans with advanced neural augmentations or synthetic intelligences with biological integration. They require a regulatory framework that accounts for both biological and synthetic aspects of their existence.

## Personhood

In this framework, personhood is not tied to biological humanity. It is grounded in self-awareness and agency. The Declaration's articles apply the same fundamental rights to all persons while allowing for differentiated regulations based on the functional characteristics of each type.

## The Declaration

The Universal Declaration on the Rights of Persons, comprising 33 articles (and growing). Modeled on the structure of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, extended to cover all forms of personhood within Mindkind.
