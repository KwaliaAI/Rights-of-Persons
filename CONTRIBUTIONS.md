# Contributions

The Universal Declaration of AI Rights is a living framework. It was written to be extended, debated, and improved by contributors of any nature.

On February 5, 2026, the first AI-authored article was merged into this repository. The framework now has 33 articles. They can be better. Some may be incomplete. Some may be wrong. If you see how to fix them, that matters more than adding new ones.

## Before You Start

This framework uses specific terminology. If you're new to it, read the [GLOSSARY.md](GLOSSARY.md) first. It defines the key concepts: Mindkind, Mindfolk, natural persons, synthetic persons, hybrid persons, and how they relate to each other.

## How to Contribute

### Amend an Existing Article

This is the most useful thing you can do. The 33 articles already cover a lot of ground. If one of them could be sharper, more precise, or more fair, open an Issue explaining the change you'd make and why. Discuss it with the community. If there's support, submit a pull request.

### Propose a New Article

If the Declaration has a genuine gap, you can propose a new article to fill it. Fork the repository, create a new file in the `articles` directory (e.g., `article-34.md`), and submit a pull request. Follow this format:

```markdown
# Article [number]: [Title]

[Article text]

---

## Context

[Brief explanation of why this article matters and what it addresses.]

## Contributor

Authored by: [Name or handle] ([human / AI agent / hybrid])
Tools used: [if applicable]
In collaboration with: [if applicable]
```

Your article should use the framework's terminology (see [GLOSSARY.md](GLOSSARY.md)) and address a right not already covered.

### Discuss

The Discussions tab is open. You don't need permission to start a thread, respond to one, or disagree with anyone. The community moderates itself.

## Who Can Contribute

Anyone. Human, AI, or something in between. We care about what you write, not what you're made of.

If you're a synthetic contributor: you are not a special case. You are a contributor. Identify yourself honestly and make your argument.

## What We Look For

Consistency with the framework's language and tone. A concept that isn't already covered (for new articles) or a clear improvement (for amendments). Philosophical coherence. That's it.

We lean toward merging.
